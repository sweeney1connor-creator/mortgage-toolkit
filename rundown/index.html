The lead

Sacramento just found its balance

For the first time this year, buyers and sellers are standing on the same step. Here is what that means for you, whichever side of the door you're on.

October was the month Sacramento County stopped being a seller's market and became a fair fight. Supply reached 3.1 months, past the three-month line the Sacramento Association of Realtors uses to call a market balanced. It had not crossed that line all year.

The median home sold for $530,000. That is 4.5% under September and 3.6% under last October. Homes took 36 days to sell, and new listings fell to 1,293, the fewest since January.

If you're selling, you still get paid. You just might have to vacuum first. If you're buying, you get something 2026 had been stingy with: time to read the inspection report before you decide.

Nobody is winning by a landslide, and that is the good news. Balanced markets are where ordinary people get deals done.

Prices

The summer high wore off, gently

June was the peak at $575,000. Since then the median has drifted down $45,000, with a small bounce in late summer. The Realtors' own six-month read calls the trend broadly flat, which is economist for "everybody calm down."

Some of this is the calendar. Fewer families move in October, and the homes that do sell skew smaller. Press play to watch the year unfold.

The agent desk

For the Realtors reading over coffee

A balanced market rewards the agents who do the unglamorous parts well. Four things worth saying out loud to clients this month:

  • Price for November, not June. The median is $45,000 off its summer peak. The comps your seller remembers are from a different season.
  • Thirty-six days is normal. A listing in week four is not stale. Set that expectation at the listing appointment and you'll get fewer 9 p.m. texts.
  • Credits are back on the table. With more than three months of supply, sellers have room to negotiate. A credit toward the buyer's rate can sometimes move the payment more than the same dollars off the price. Have your lender run both.
  • Your buyers have time. Use it. Inspections, second showings and a full night's sleep are all available again.

Small business win

From a tent to a storefront

Tola Vong and Chhayleang Tang started selling cinnamon rolls from a farmers market pop-up this summer. Their first shop, Cinnapop Cafe, is planned for a 500-square-foot space at 611 16th St. in Midtown, with a soft opening this month. Flavors include ube and matcha.

Small business win

Midtown keeps adding doors

Midtown gained 21 new businesses in 2024 and 24 in 2025, and CBS Sacramento reported early this year that 2026 was on the same pace. East Village Book Shop opened a second location on J Street.

Random fun fact

Sacramento once jacked itself up

After the floods of the early 1860s, the city raised its downtown streets by roughly ten feet, lifting some buildings and turning other ground floors into basements. Parts of the old street level are still down there under Old Sacramento. This town has a history of rising above things.

Looking ahead

Reasons to like where this is going

Rates have eased for three straight weeks. Buyers have more to choose from than at any point this year. Prices are holding roughly where they were a year ago instead of sprinting away from paychecks. None of that is a forecast, and none of it is nothing.

Winter is the quiet season, which makes it a good time to get your numbers in order. Spring always shows up in Sacramento. It brings allergies, but it shows up.
